# How I made this

I used AI tools for a lot of the drafting, diagrams, layout, and file production. I did not hand-write every sentence or draw every diagram.

My part was the learning problem, the sequence, the constraints, the critique, and the call on what was good enough to keep. When a draft looked polished but would not help someone actually learn the topic, I said what was wrong, gave a better way to think about it, and asked for another pass. The rules that kept working became the templates and rubrics in this repo.

## What I don't claim

- I did not train or fine-tune any model. Better output came from better instructions and review, not from changing a model.
- I'm not the sole author of every page or the engineer behind every file.
- These materials show a method. They don't prove learning gains yet.

## What counts as my contribution

I log a change when it moves one of these:

- how the problem is defined
- the teaching sequence
- a factual or safety constraint
- how quality is judged
- a diagram or visual explanation
- how gaps get repaired
- what gets kept or thrown out
- a workflow I can reuse

"Make it better" doesn't count. A specific critique that changes the result in a way I can repeat does.
